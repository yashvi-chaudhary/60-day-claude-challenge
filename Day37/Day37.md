🧭 Day 37 of #60DayClaudeChallenge: Task Compass

Today's build wasn't about testing job titles. It was about teaching something most of us learn the hard way on the job — ownership, delegation, escalation, and collaboration.

What I built:
 A single-file HTML simulation where you play three stages as work moves through a tech company:

→ Stage 1 — Who Owns This? Drag a role into the ownership slot for a real workplace scenario, then see the reasoning (not just right/wrong)
 → Stage 2 — Task Routing Build the actual workflow a ticket moves through, from intake to resolution
 → Stage 3 — Collaboration Challenge Assign up to 4 roles to bigger, messier problems where no single owner is enough

At the end, instead of a score, you get a radar chart across four skills — Ownership, Delegation, Collaboration, Workflow Thinking — and an honest reflection on where you over-assigned responsibility or underestimated how many people a real problem actually touches.

The uncomfortable finding:
 Building the reasoning logic for Stage 1 made me realize how easy it is to default to "whoever's closest to the bug" instead of "whoever actually owns the fix." A frontend crash on iPhone feels like a frontend problem — but tracing the reasoning through each scenario reminded me that ownership isn't about proximity, it's about accountability for the outcome.

Stack: Pure HTML/CSS/Vanilla JS, drag-and-drop, animated workflow routing, and a hand-rolled Canvas radar chart — no frameworks, fully offline
Many real workplace problems are solved by teams, not individuals. This one tries to make that lesson feel like a game instead of a lecture.
