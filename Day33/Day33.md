Day 33/60 of #60DayClaudeChallenge 🧠

Today's build tackles something that affects literally everyone with a phone: misleading headlines and emotional manipulation.

I built a Media Integrity Analyzer — a fully interactive, single-file HTML app that teaches media literacy through discovery, not testing.

Here's how it works:
✅ Headline Detective — you're shown a realistic fake headline + article, asked "would you click this?", then asked to flag the exaggerated phrases yourself before the analysis reveals the Headline Accuracy Score, the mismatches, and a fair rewritten version
✅ Emotion Detector — a fictional social post is shown, you describe how it made you feel and which words did it, then the reveal breaks down the target audience, the manipulation technique, and a neutral rewrite
✅ Live Media Integrity metrics — Headline Accuracy, Source Reliability, Emotional Manipulation, Audience Targeting — updating as you go
✅ A final Media Integrity Dashboard — overall score, what you learned, your biggest red flag, and 3 practical habits to take with you
The uncomfortable part I ran into building this: it's genuinely hard to write "fair" headlines and "neutral" rewrites without them feeling flat. That tension — accuracy vs. engagement — is exactly the problem this app is trying to teach people to notice. Living the lesson while building it.

Zero backend. Zero libraries. Pure HTML/CSS/JS. Runs completely offline.
