Day 34/60 of #60DayClaudeChallenge 🕵️‍♀️

Today's build: a full detective game, not a dashboard.

"Marketing Detective" — you're handed a fictional company's campaign case file (18 of them, randomly assigned), and you have to investigate why it underperformed before you can close the case.

What I built: 
✅ A corkboard-style investigation board with draggable evidence (sticky notes, push pins, the whole aesthetic) 
✅ 18 detailed fictional cases — each with full metrics, customer comments, social performance, budget allocation, and a hidden root-cause mistake 
✅ An accusation stage with multiple-choice suspects + an optional hint system 
✅ A "Case Closed" stamp animation, followed by a full Learning Report breaking down the real explanation and suggested fixes 
✅ All built single-file, React + Babel via CDN, zero backend, zero npm

The uncomfortable part: writing 18 plausible marketing failures was harder than writing the UI. It's easy to write "the ad was bad." It's much harder to write a case where the metrics look fine (high CTR, strong reach) and the actual mistake is buried in a customer comment or a budget split — because that's what real diagnostic thinking requires. A few of my first drafts were too obvious. Had to rewrite them so the "aha" moment actually requires connecting evidence, not just reading the biggest red flag.
Lesson for today: good instructional design and good detective fiction use the same skill — controlled reveal. If the answer is obvious from clue #1, there's no mystery. If it's buried in clue #3 and only makes 
sense next to the customer comments, you've built something worth solving.
