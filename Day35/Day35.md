Day 35/60 of #60DayClaudeChallenge 🧩

Today I built something meta: a game that teaches you how to prompt better — by making you play with prompts.

🧩 Prompt Puzzle — Master AI Prompting Through Play
I picked Data Analysis & Business as the domain and built a full gamified trainer, single HTML file, works completely offline.

What's inside: 
✅ 6 randomized business scenarios — sales drops, churn analysis, A/B test verdicts, competitor pricing, KPI anomalies, budget reallocation 
✅ 3 challenge types: Build the Prompt (drag-and-drop), Clean the Prompt (spot the junk), Choose the Best Prompt (weak vs optimized vs over-engineered) 
✅ Live scoring across accuracy, time, moves, wrong placements, hints used, and an optimization bonus 
✅ A full Prompt Performance Report — score, rank, Prompt DNA breakdown, personalized feedback, and your best optimized prompt

The uncomfortable finding: 
building the "over-engineered" prompt examples was almost harder than the optimized ones. It's easy to think more instructions = better output. 
Watching side-by-side how a bloated prompt actually buries the real ask — vs. a tight Role → Context → Task → Format → Constraint prompt — was the real lesson today.

Lesson: good prompting isn't about saying more. It's about saying the right things, in the right structure, and stopping there.
